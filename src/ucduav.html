About the Team

The UCD UAV team was founded early in 2024 and recruiting had just begun. I joined the team in February of 2024 under the aeromechanical team. The team consists of 10 students ranging in mechanical and electrical/electronic engineering, and our academic advisor Kevin Nolan. There are three branches in the team; aeromechanical, avionics, and business/social media.

My role on the team spanned from simulations to aeromechanical and manufacturing.

My Role on the Team

Throughout my time at the UCD UAV team, I have worked closely with both the aeromechanical and avionics departments, ensuring clear communication happens between the two. In the beginning, I focused on simulations, particularly modelling our UAV in the software known as X-Plane 11. Later on I worked on integrating X-Plane to mission planner in Ardupilot. I then worked on Finite Element Analysis (FEA) specifically for the landing gears of the UAV. Finally, I developed and built the ground control station (GCS) which acted as the storage unit for the UAV and also served as an operating station while preparing to fly, and during flight.

X-Plane

I developed a fully functional flight simulation model of our UAV using X-Plane 11 and Plane Maker. This allowed us to validate our design decisions, calculations and flight performance.

Key contributions achieved:
Built several custom UAV prototypes in Plane Maker: I did this by replicating the geometry of the plane based off CAD models, creating and sizing control surfaces, inputting propulsion system characteristics, and mass properties based off calculations.

Dynamics Validation: X-Plane was used to validate the plane's flying performance to verify if the aerodynamic coefficients and control surfaces along with their deflections ensured that the UAV was operable. I was able to modify the environmental settings by changing cross-wind on takeoff etc, to see how the UAV would react. From these tests, we found out that the UAV was able to produce a lot of lift on takeoff, therefore requiring a short takeoff distance, but it was also slightly statically unstable in roll, meaning that the UAV failed to correct and return to level flight after being slightly distrubed in roll.

I used X-Plane to simulate the effects of dropping our 2kg payload. I was able to conclude that while the UAV became more maneuverable, there weren't any sudden effects as the centre of gravity (COG) of the payload was roughly directly below the COG of the whole UAV, therefore the longitudinal location of the COG did not change when the payload was dropped. The payload drop itself was modelled by a jettison water drop in X-Plane.
