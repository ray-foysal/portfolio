About the Team

The Formula Student project is UCD's motorsport engineering team. We are tasked with designing, building, and racing a fully electric single seat car for the IMechE Formula Student UK competition held at Silverstone. The project is fully student led and brings engineering students from all years and disciplines to tackle this project.

The team is structured into three main departments:

Powertrain: focused on the high voltage system and battery, motor, and control electronics

Mechanical: responsible for the chassis, suspension, aerodynamics, and ergonomics

Operations: manages finance, outreaches to potential sponsors, documentation, and team logistics

My Role on the Team

I joined the UCD Formula Student team at the beginning of the 2023/24 season, during my 1st year of engineering at UCD, and became a member of the Powertrain Department. Although my background is in mechanical engineering, I was eager to expand my knowledge and gain hands on experience in electrical systems, particularly in high-voltage. This interdisciplinary approach also proved to be highly valuable for the team, as it strengthened communication and collaboration between the mechanical and electrical departments during design phases.

As of rigth now, I am part of the High Voltage Subsystem team, specifically working on the Tractive System Accumulator Control (TSAC). My responsibilities included:

Soldering circuit components using KiCAD to reference the location and component part.

Defining fusing requirements for each circuit based on expected current and analysis of the potential fault scenarios from most likely to most extreme. Looking at research papers to validate our calculations.

Researching potential fuses for our system and coordinating with our academic advisor to ensure we took the right steps and always considering safety.

Contributing to the electrical safety documentation required by our college and academic supervisor, as well as having it as a reference while building the TSAC.

Collaborating with mechanical teammates to ensure efficient cooling and layout inside the accumulator container and on the chasis.

Assisting in CAD modelling of electrical assemblies and creating the new version of the TSAC to house the battery cells.

Challenges & Solutions

We faced a number of obstacles throughout the years:

Rule compliance: The FS rules for electrical systems are strict. We spent time ensuring all our design fit within the regulations specified in the rulebook.

Thermal buildup:Battery modules needed airflow paths and heat-resistant insulation. We used epoxy coating on the inside of the TSAC to isolate each battery pack from each other. A passive cooling system was used to channel cold air from side ducts into the TSAC to cool the batteries. Active cooling such as using fans was also researched.

Team handover: With many team members graduating and moving on from the team each year, documenting work for future members became crucial. We had faced major problems especially with getting the motor resolver to communicate with the controller because wires had been modified and cut by previous team members with no explanation as to why they did it.

Battery Assembly Approval: One of our biggest challenges was getting permission to build and assemble our high voltage battery pack. For two years, we worked closely with our academic supervisor for powertrain who represented the university. Despite presenting detailed plans and iterating on multiple versions of our fusing and safety documentation, the college ultimately did not deem it safe for us to construct the battery on campus.
As a result, we were forced to outsource the battery build externally. While this allowed us to meet deadlines, it was disheartening after years of effort proving we could design, assemble, and operate the battery with appropriate safety measures in place, including fuses and high voltage isolations, fault detection, and emergency shutoffs.

Results

In the 2025 Formula Student UK competition at Silverstone, we finished 28th out of 59 teams. We were very proud to have brought our car the closest it has ever been to actually racing as an EV for our team. This is a huge milestone after years of effort and iteration.

This season laid a solid foundation for 2026. With many of our major powertrain challenges now resolved, including the battery assembly, circuit boards designed and manufactured, and a working powertrain, we're entering the next competition year with more experience, a better tested system, and a clearer path forward.

The team is now in a very strong position to climb the rankings and showcase the full potential of our engineering efforts, so stay tuned to find out how we do.
