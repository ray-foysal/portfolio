RoboWars
Designed to flip, fight, and win - our combat robot was engineered by a passionate team of students for the Dogpatch RoboWars 2025 arena.

About the Project

The RoboWars project was created for the RoboWars Fight Night 2025 held at Dogpatch Labs in Dublin. The competition required teams to build and battle custom designed robots in an arena. Our robot was designed to be a flipper bot. It focused on aggression and speed to launch opponents out of the arena and deal heavy blows, while also being very resilient with a heavy armour made from sheets of titanium and aluminium.

Design & Mechanism

Our bot featured a pneumatic flipper system powered by a CO₂ canister and a solenoid valve, actuating a heavy duty 32mm bore piston that could deliver rapid and forceful flips. The chassis was made from 3d printed PLA Metal and PETG, and reinforced with aluminium for a very strong yet lightweight frame, and the armor used titanium and aluminium for protection. Key design elements:

Drive system: 2WD powered by brushed drill motors that featured a two stage planetary gearbox.

Armour: angled plates of armour to deflect hits

Weapon: front-mounted flipper with reinforced hinge points and titanium protection.

Mobility: low ground clearance for stability, control, low centre of gravity and protection against other bots striking from underneath.

Challenges Faced

We encountered several engineering hurdles:

Material stress: Components near the piston would deform under repeated impacts, requiring reinforcement. A lot of 3d printed parts also fractured under huge amounts of shock and stress, therefore they had to be redesigned in a way that they could distribute those stresses away.

Weight distribution & Forces: The force kickback from actuating the piston caused our whole bot to rock backwards. To counter this force, we had to shift components around to achieve a centre of gravity further forward. We also added small castor wheels underneath the bot to provide stability when actuating the piston and to allow the bot to balance on only 2 main wheels.

Regulations: Meeting the strict safety constraints of RoboWars while packing in high pressure pneumatics was difficult. We also had a weight limit of 7kg that we quickly reached and had to optimise our bot for.

Battery Management On the day of the competition, we had a huge issues with our batteries. After plugging in our battery pack of 4S2P Li-ions, we faced current surges that fried components on board. This issue was luckily resolved by replacing the batteries with those of a different brand. Another issue with our batteries was that when the bot took a hit, the batteries would shift and become disconnected, which disabled our whole bot. We fixed this by adding in small nuts to tension the batteries in their holders so that they would be very hard to dislodge.

Learning Outcomes

Learning Outcomes:

Gained hands on experience with pneumatic systems and their control.
Learned to iterate mechanical designs quickly under tight deadlines.
Improved skills in team based design, CAD integration, and manufacturing using power tools especially with aluminium and titanium.
Learned how to think creatively under extreme pressure, optimising within rules while still meeting tight deadlines.
